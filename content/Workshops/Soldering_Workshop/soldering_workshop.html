Safety First

Soldering irons run at 600-700°F (315-370°C) - hot enough to cause a serious burn instantly, and the fumes are worth respecting too.

  • Always assume the iron is hot. Never touch the metal tip or barrel.
  • Try to keep your head out of the fumes.
  • Keep the iron in its stand whenever you’re not actively soldering. Never set it down on the table.
  • Tie back long hair and avoid loose sleeves near the tip.
  • Wash your hands after handling solder and before eating or touching your face.

Tools & Materials

You’ll be given your soldering kits. These will be included:

  • Soldering iron (temperature-controlled, ~600-650°F for this project)
  • Solder wire (thin, rosin-core)
  • Perf board
  • “Helping hands” to hold the board steady

Provided by the Lab:

  • Safety glasses
  • Tip Cleaners
  • Wire Cutters and Stripers

Basic Technique

  1. Tin the tip. Melt a small amount of solder directly onto a freshly-cleaned tip - this helps heat transfer and keeps the tip from oxidizing.
  2. Heat the joint, not the solder. Touch the iron to both the component lead and the pad at the same time, and hold it there for 1-2 seconds.
  3. Feed the solder into the joint (not onto the iron tip) and let it melt and flow around the lead and pad.
  4. Remove the solder, then the iron. Pull the solder away first, then lift the iron. The whole joint should take about 2-3 seconds total.
  5. Let it cool undisturbed for a few seconds - moving the joint while it solidifies causes a “cold joint” that looks dull/grainy and can fail electrically.

A good joint is shiny, smooth, and shaped like a small volcano/cone around the lead. A cold joint looks dull, lumpy, or cracked - if you see one, just reheat it and let it re-flow. The Adafruit guide linked below has good reference photos of both.
