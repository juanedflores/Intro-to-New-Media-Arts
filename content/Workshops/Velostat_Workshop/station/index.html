Sensor Grid

Squeeze together

Setting up the stations

Four Arduino Nano 33 IoT boards, five sensors each: 20 sensors in all. Every sensor becomes the top half of a voltage divider, and the board reads the voltage in the middle. The boards send their readings over Wi-Fi through the classroom router to this laptop, so there are no cables to the computer.

3.3V only. The Nano 33 IoT's pins can't take more than 3.3V. Power the sensors from the board's 3.3V pin, never from 5V or the breadboard power module. Take the power module off the station breadboards.

The router (once)

  1. Plug in the router. It doesn't need the internet. Make sure its 2.4 GHz network is on (the Nano can't use 5 GHz) and has a name and a WPA2 password.

Each station (once)

  1. In the pressure_station folder, put the router's 2.4 GHz name and password in arduino_secrets.h.
  2. Open pressure_station.ino, set STATION to 1, 2, 3 or 4, upload, and label the board with that number.
  3. On the station breadboard: wire the Nano's 3.3V pin to the + rail and GND to the – rail.
  4. Put five 10kΩ resistors on the board, each from its own row to the – rail. Run a jumper from A0, A1, A2, A3, A4 to those five rows, and label each row with masking tape.
  5. Power the board from a USB power bank or phone charger. Its built-in LED blinks while joining the Wi-Fi and stays on once connected.

In class

  1. Join the laptop to the router's network.
  2. Double-click start_sensor_grid.command in the station folder. A Terminal window opens and this page opens in your browser. The Terminal lists each station as it connects. (The first time, macOS may ask whether Python can accept incoming connections: click Allow.)
  3. Each station's label on the left should say Wi-Fi.

Backup: USB

The same boards also send over USB. If the Wi-Fi gives trouble, plug a station into the laptop, open this page in Chrome or Edge and click Connect station (USB). No re-upload needed.

Students

  1. Clip one sensor lead to the station's + rail and the other to an open labeled row.
  2. Find your tile, click its label and type your name. Then squeeze!

Keys

T test mode (hold 1–5, Q–T, A–G, Z–B, one row per station, or hold a tile with the mouse) · R recalibrate · F full screen · ? this help

Each tile learns its own sensor: the lowest reading is "resting" and the highest is "pressed," so a soft sensor and a stiff one both fill their tile. A tile with nothing plugged in reads 0 (the 10kΩ resistor pulls it to ground) and shows as empty.
